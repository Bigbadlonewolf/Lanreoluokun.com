---
title: "Why banks should treat cloud IAM like credit risk"
date: 2026-07-03
summary: "Access is a loan. The bank underwrites it, monitors it, and calls it in when the risk changes."
---

In retail banking, a loan officer does not hand a customer a limit and walk away. The bank checks income, sets a ceiling, reviews the account, and reduces or closes the line when behavior changes. Cloud IAM should work the same way.

Most banks still treat cloud access as a provisioning ticket. A developer asks for a role, someone approves it, and the grant stays in place until the employee leaves. That is the equivalent of issuing a perpetual loan with no covenants and no review date.

The alternative is just-in-time, conditional access. A user gets a role for a limited window, for a specific scope, with an audit trail attached. The conditions act like loan covenants: break them and the access is revoked automatically.

Banks already have the discipline. Credit committees review risk appetite monthly. The same rigor can be applied to cloud permissions. The result is fewer standing admin grants, smaller blast radius, and an audit story that regulators can follow without a translator.
